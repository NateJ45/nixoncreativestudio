# D. Copy, story and positioning audit (redesign 2026)

Agent D, 2026-10-03. Read-only audit: nothing in the repo or CMS was edited. Every quote below was read in context on the live site (fetched 2026-10-03) and in the committed fallback copy. Where a line lives only in the CMS database (case studies), the location is given as the live URL plus field.

## Headline findings (read this first)

1. **The voice is mostly fine. The honesty is the problem.** Earlier critiques (June 19 and June 21) were right that the copy is plain and human. What makes the site feel generic is not banned words; it is that the same six phrases repeat on every page and that several claims are untrue or unproven. On the live site: "start to finish" appears 14 times, "front door" 21, "honest" 12, "actually" 23, "pace it together" 12. Repetition at that density reads as a template, and the untrue claims are a real risk with a referral network that talks to each other.
2. **Four case studies describe things that are not live.** These are verified, not guessed:
   - **Second Presbyterian Church of Chicago** is presented as "Live at secondpreschicago.org", and its screenshot leads the home hero. That domain still serves Squarespace (curl, 2026-10-03: 150+ "squarespace" markers). The vault note says "Build complete but never launched" and the client went silent. "Anyone on the communications team can now add a sermon" is false.
   - **Reid Design** describes a Budget Calculator, Style Quiz, before/after sliders, journal, shop and press strip. On reiddesignllc.com, /calculator/ and /quiz/ redirect to /services/, /journal/ and /shop/ redirect home, the portfolio is empty, and the vault records "the Before & After removal" as live. The outcome line ("turns browsers into qualified consultation requests") has no evidence: the September report shows 39 sessions in the month.
   - **The Presbyterian Academy** claims "real faculty, honest pricing" and a "Testimonials section seeded from early cohort participants". Nathan said on 2026-10-03 that much of the live faculty, tuition and term content is placeholder seed. The designer's note says publishing prices "drew the people who were actually ready to think about enrolling": no evidence exists.
   - **First Presbyterian Church of Orangeburg** says "the coordination time dropped a lot". Nothing measures it, and the vault says the site has not been worked on in years.
3. **The /work page promise is broken by item 2.** "Every project here is a real, shipped site. Do not take my word for it, click through and look around." A visitor who clicks secondpreschicago.org lands on a Squarespace site that is not Nathan's.
4. **The Crestview metric could not be found.** No source in the repo, the vault or the archive mentions Crestview doubling visit-planning form submissions. Crestview's website work is a proposal on hold; Nathan's Crestview work is volunteer AV and media. **Do not repeat this claim** unless Nathan produces the source.
5. **No client has ever been quoted on the site, and no testimonial exists for Nathan anywhere on record.** The About page's "What clients say" block is wired up but renders nothing. Reid Design's 5.0 Google rating is Staci's business, not Nathan's.

## 2. Positioning

### Who buys (ranked by likelihood to buy, then budget)

1. **Small Cincinnati-region nonprofits and event organizations run by one director or volunteers** (the Stone Steps shape). Likely: local and reachable in person, and Stone Steps is a paid build with the strongest story. Budget $4k to $9k. The best target.
2. **Scholarly and church-adjacent publications and foundations** (FRT and Theology Matters, through Dr. Burnett). Proven to pay ($5,000 FRT build plus a support plan) and content-heavy, so the self-publishing pitch is worth real money. Budget $5k to $12k plus Care. Risk: referrals arrive anchored at "$5,000" (vault).
3. **Churches, regionally and through the Presbyterian network.** The deepest portfolio (four sites), but the vault shows this network has seen Nathan work for $0 to $5,000. Likely to inquire, harder to price. Budget $4k to $8k.
4. **Owner-run small businesses leaving Squarespace or Wix** (MAS, Reid). The "you own it, no monthly rent" argument lands, but they are price-sensitive and both examples were free or low-fee. Budget $4k to $7k.
5. **Schools.** Lowest. The WCP board declined the cutover specifically because it wants an outside company for continuity. A one-person studio must answer the "what if you disappear" question before schools will buy.

### The one thing he does better

**Nathan builds sites where each fact is entered once and stays right without a webmaster.** Stone Steps calculates its records from 2,188 checked finishes and updates itself the morning after each race. The Second Pres build keeps the service time in one field. Theology Matters puts audio on every article automatically. A template shop hands you a blank editor and the data drifts. An agency does this kind of work but charges for a team and a retainer. Nathan does it as one accountable person and hands it over.

### The unfair advantage (what is actually true)

- One person plans, designs and builds every site (true for all nine). Photography is a real extra for local clients, not a universal claim.
- An engineer's habit of checking facts, already shown in the case studies: the 1:58:36 duplicate, 12 unsupported "records" removed, the 10,726 ft claim against the LiDAR-measured ~5,200 ft.
- A studio pipeline most small organizations never get: an accessibility gate on every change, automated encrypted backups, uptime and DNS watching (the studio-status dashboard), monthly analytics reports (the August and September reports exist), and a shared starter so each fix improves every site.
- Ownership: the custom-coded builds run on Cloudflare at little or no monthly cost, and the client owns them outright.

### Honest limits (do not claim)

- "Every site photographed by me." "Sites that score 100." Only accessibility 100 is gated. This site shows Performance 92, the Stone Steps median was 80 to 88 at one point, and FRT is the one recorded 100/100/100/100.
- Any conversion or lead outcome. Nothing is measured, and the studio's own GA4 tag has been dead since June.
- "Most projects land between $7,000 and $11,000" (never transacted).
- That the portfolio is "around Cincinnati", that it is all live, or the Crestview metric.
- Scale or continuity he cannot back up. Answer the WCP objection instead: handoff docs, backups, Care.

## 3. Positioning statement and headline directions

**Positioning statement.** Nixon Creative Studio builds websites for churches, nonprofits and small organizations that the people already there can run and keep accurate. Nathan Nixon plans, designs and builds each one himself, enters each fact once so it cannot drift, and hands over a site you own outright, with the backups and checks small organizations rarely get.

### Direction A: "Keeps its own facts straight"

- **Headline:** Websites that keep their own facts straight.
- **Sub-line:** Service times, results, prices and dates go in once, so your site never contradicts itself. Planned, designed and built by Nathan Nixon in Cincinnati.
- **Above-the-fold promise:** an accurate site without a webmaster.
- **Proof it leans on:** Stone Steps (2,188 finishes, records calculated from them, a nightly import, the LiDAR-measured climb) as the lead case study with its before and after. Theology Matters (audio on every article, 149 of 154 posts linked to their editions).
- **Risk:** reads technical or dry to a buyer shopping for looks, and it leans heavily on one case study. Do not cite Second Pres's single-sourced service time as an outcome until that site launches.

### Direction B: "Your next volunteer can run it"

- **Headline:** A website your next volunteer can run.
- **Sub-line:** I plan it, design it and build it myself, then hand it over with an editor your team can use, a written guide and backups that are checked. You own it outright.
- **Above-the-fold promise:** no lock-in, no lost passwords, and no rebuild when the volunteer who ran it moves on.
- **Proof it leans on:** FRT and Theology Matters staff publish without a developer; Mary Ann edits MAS herself; the backup, uptime and accessibility pipeline; ownership with little or no monthly cost.
- **Risk:** it walks straight into the continuity objection that cost WCP. It only works if a Care plan and a documented handoff sit right beside it. "$0 a month" is not true for the Wix builds, so scope that claim.

### Direction C: "One person, and he picks up next year"

- **Headline:** One person plans it, builds it, and still answers the phone next year.
- **Sub-line:** Nathan Nixon, Cincinnati. Websites for churches, nonprofits, schools and small businesses, from $4,000.
- **Above-the-fold promise:** one accountable person, with no handoffs between strategist, designer and developer.
- **Proof it leans on:** the About story and the day-one line; Stone Steps, a 2024 build Nathan came back to rebuild two years later; ongoing FRT and Theology Matters support since spring 2026.
- **Risk:** it needs a client quote to land ("he still picks up" has to come from a client, not from Nathan), and it invites the "what if you are sick" question. Without a testimonial it is just a self-claim.

Recommendation: lead with **A's proof and B's promise**. Use B's headline, and make Stone Steps the hero image and first case study. Hold C until Nathan has two real quotes.
