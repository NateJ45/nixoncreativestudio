# D9: a ground for the d7a hero

Built 2026-10-04 after Nathan called the plain cream ground of d7a and d1 an AI tell. The brief: texture, movement, grace and classiness; a ground that carries the studio's identity and feels like a physical material or a real place, expensive and restrained, not decorative. The honest trap: grain-gradient mesh, blurry aurora blobs, dark navy glow and noise-over-gradient are themselves today's tells.

**Files.** `index.html` is the study page (a switcher over two live frames, 1440 and 390, of each ground). Every ground is also its own page so it can be measured alone: `t0.html` (control, d7a's flat cream), `t1.html` to `t8.html`, plus `t0-logo-top.html` and `t3-logo-top.html` (the logo test below). `assets/` holds the baked grounds and the logo WebPs; type, client frames and fonts are referenced from `../d7a-carousel-contact-sheet/`. `shots/` holds every screenshot and the measurements (`contrast.json`, `perf.json`, `lighthouse-mobile.json`, `weight.json`, `logo.json`). `_build/` holds the scripts (run from MAIN): `bake.mjs` (all raster grounds), `topo.mjs` (contour SVG), `logo.mjs`, `treatments.mjs` (each ground's layers and CSS), `build.mjs` (generates every page from d7a's own `index.html`), `shoot.mjs` (screenshots plus contrast), `sheen.mjs`, `perf.mjs`, `lh.mjs`, `weight.mjs`, `index-shot.mjs`, `serve.mjs`, `zoom.mjs`. Nothing in the repo source, CMS or production was touched; nothing is committed.

**What every page shares.** d7a's inline CSS, header, hero and carousel script verbatim, with Nathan's logo in place of the N-mark lockup, wrapped in a `.stage` that owns the ground. Grounds animate `transform` or `opacity` only; `will-change` sits only on the layers that move. A 0.3 KB IntersectionObserver adds `.is-off` to pause every ground animation off screen (verified: all ground animations report `paused` when the stage is scrolled away). Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` no ground animation runs (verified: 0 running on every page) and each shows a designed still (`shots/*__reduced.png`). Nothing in the first viewport starts at opacity 0.

## For a church board or a preschool director

1. **T8, window light.** Reads as a real person in a real room; warm, light, calm, nothing to decode. A board member sees a studio, not an effect. It keeps the light page Nathan's clients already trust and the red italic at full strength.
2. **T5, the Ohio bend.** Local and specific without saying so: a Cincinnati board member recognises the river; an out-of-town client reads "a real place". Quiet behind text (body copy 6.5:1 at its worst pixel).
3. **T1, the board,** only for the hero band of a single page (or a case study opener) if Nathan wants one dark, expensive moment. On a phone it is handsome, but green and gold can read "out of our budget" before the $4,000 line.

T2, T3 and T7 read as decorative or moody to these buyers; the reviewer's persona pass called T3 "a nightclub or a moody agency, the opposite of calm, clear, on time".

Every page: accessibility 100, CLS 0 (T5 0.002), TBT 0 ms. LCP element: the centre Stone Steps frame, as in d7a. Logo: 5.9 KB (440 px) or 9.1 KB (640 px, 2x desktop), the same on every page including the control.

How to read it:

- **Lighthouse barely moves.** Every ground costs one simulated step of LCP (1.96 to 2.18 s, 2.10 s for T6), whatever its size: the ground images are requested alongside the LCP frame and share the simulated link. Mobile score holds at 99; one run of T4 gave 97. All stay inside C's budget (95+, LCP 2.5 s). To claw back the 0.2 s, load the ground images after `load` (the colour token paints first; the texture is low-contrast so the swap is quiet).
- **No repaints while animating.** The trace shows the same 2 paints as the control during 6 s of animation: the grounds are composited layers moved by the compositor, as intended.
- **Main thread.** Running compositor animations still tick style on the main thread: about +120 to +150 ms of style per 6 s at 4x throttle (2 to 2.5% of a throttled core). T6 (scroll-driven, idle when not scrolling) costs nothing.
- **GPU is the real cost, and this machine overstates it.** Headless Chromium here draws with SwiftShader (software Vulkan), so the last column is CPU-rendered compositing: every animating ground redraws the full-bleed stage each frame (361 draws in 6 s against 13 for the control). Frame pacing held at 60 fps with 0 dropped frames everywhere, but the ranking is real fill-rate cost: T7 (three large layers, one rotated) is heaviest, T5 and T2 next, T1, T4 and T8 lightest of the moving ones. On a real phone GPU these are cheap; on a low-end phone, two full-screen moving layers is the most I would allow.

## Contrast (WCAG 2, measured on the moving ground)

Method (`shoot.mjs`): at 8 points of each loop, text is hidden, the ground is screenshotted, and every text run's box is scanned pixel by pixel against the text colour. "Worst" is the single worst pixel; "p1" is the 1st percentile. Text on solid fills (buttons, film strip, the "Launching soon" tag at 4.75:1) is computed from its own fill. Large text needs 3:1, the rest 4.5:1.

## Nathan's logo

Used on every page: `logo-navy` on light grounds, `logo-white` on T1 to T3. The artwork is one flat colour (navy `#0a1628`) over soft anti-aliased alpha; the WebPs keep the alpha (stepped to 32 levels, invisible) and the colour exactly, and were checked with a composite-difference test (0 levels). No recolouring, no trace (the soft edges would not survive an SVG trace identically). Sizes: 5.9 KB at 440 px, 9.1 KB at 640 px, 3.0 KB for the top line alone.

Displayed 236 px wide on desktop (the small "WEB DESIGN | PHOTOGRAPHY | STRATEGY" line is about 14 CSS px tall) and 212 px on a 390 phone (about 12.7 px). Both are legible in the screenshots, so I kept the full lockup; the top-line-only crop (`t0-logo-top.html`, `t3-logo-top.html`, `shots/t0-logo-top__390__f1.png`) is there if Nathan prefers a quieter header on phones, at 190 px. Worst logo contrast on any ground at its worst moment: 4.9:1 at 390 on T2 before the flat tint, 6.6:1 after the final changes (T1); all others 8.6 to 14.8.

## Impeccable critique (dual-agent) and what changed

Method: dual-agent (A: isolated design-review sub-agent on the default model; B: isolated detector sub-agent on Sonnet). Browser overlay skipped (static files, no dev server left running); nothing written to `.impeccable/`. The user-question step was skipped because this ran as a delegated study; the questions are in "What it needs from Nathan".

- **A, design review,** answered the question directly: owned = T5 and T8; borderline = T1, T2, T4, T6; tell = T3 and T7; T0 lacks identity. Nielsen scores for the hero as a Persuade surface: 23/28 on the applicable seven (aesthetic 3 on T8, T5, T6; 2 on T2, T3, T7). Specific findings acted on: a hard seam 13 to 25 px from the right edge on T2, T5 and T7 (cause: a specificity clash stopped each layer's overscan inset, so drifting layers showed their edge; fixed with `:where()`); T8's light read as a boxed overlay and its mullions crossed the headline on phones (redrawn as a leaning parallelogram with distance-dependent penumbra, moved right of the title, phone scale so one pane holds the headline, limewash with almost no relief); T2's oval veil read as a glow behind the hero (now a flat tint) and the marbling was desaturated; T1's fibres were too large (tile drawn at 256 px) and its lamp too faint (warmer lift in the band); T4's repeat showed on phones (dye mottles added; the band mask also had a hard edge on tall screens, fixed); T5's label was clipped (moved). Not changed: T3 and T7 (their concept is the problem; they stay as evidence); A's note that the T6 impression reads embossed (the white-below, dark-above shadow is the conventional inset-text cue; left as is); the two "Start a project" buttons above the fold (d7a's).
- **B, detector:** no ground adds a material finding against the control. New per page: one `cramped-padding` on the full-bleed `.stage` (false positive); T6's sheen copy flagged as low contrast (false positive: aria-hidden decoration) and its plate shadow as a `dark-glow` (the zero-offset inner shadow was removed); hover contrast on T4, T7, T8 (real, fixed). Shared with the control: d7a's known findings (deferred images, chrome padding, italic serif display, cream palette).

## Risks

1. **T8 depends on restraint.** A window-light overlay is also a stock Instagram asset. It works here because the geometry is physical (leaning patch, penumbra widening with distance, falloff down the wall) and because it moves like the sun, slowly. Make it bigger, sharper or faster and it becomes a filter.
2. **Dark mode.** PRODUCT.md promises light and dark. T8, T5 and T6 are light ideas. T8 has a natural night version (the same wall under a warm lamp, no window); T5 inverts well (pale lines on survey-green). Decide before building.
3. **The ground fights the carousel for texture.** The carousel already carries six to seven busy screenshots; that is why the quiet grounds win. T2 and T7 make the first screen noisier.
4. **GPU fill on cheap phones.** Every moving ground redraws the full stage each frame. Keep to two moving layers, pause off screen (done), and consider stopping the loop after a few cycles.
5. **T1's palette.** Ink green and gilt drifts from the logo's navy and the red marker. If chosen, it needs a stated relationship to the brand colours, not a second brand.
6. **T5's honesty.** The river course is traced, the relief is drawn. The caption or alt-free decoration must not claim survey data.
7. **Measured on one machine** with software GPU and simulated throttling; frame pacing on a real mid-range Android is worth five minutes.

## Recommended combinations

Change ground deliberately between bands, never mid-band:

1. **Studio light (recommended).** T8 for the hero; T6 cotton stock for the proof sheet and prices bands (same warm paper family, no motion); one dark band (d7a's film black, "About: in the darkroom") as the single contrast moment; T5 contours as the ground of the contact/close band, where "Cincinnati" belongs. One moving ground per page, at the top.
2. **Place first.** T5 for the hero; T6 below; T8's light only on the About band beside Nathan's photograph.
3. **One dark, expensive page.** T1 board for the hero of a case study or the services page only, T6 paper below it. Not for the home page, where the light grounds serve the buyers better.
