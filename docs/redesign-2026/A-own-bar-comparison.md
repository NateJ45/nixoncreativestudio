## 1. Why it feels generic, ranked

Severity: P0 blocks the site from making its case, P1 a major drag on credibility, P2 a visible weakness, P3 polish.

1. **[P0] No creative idea of its own. The site's identity is a blue wash, a free display face and a tech-demo effect.** Every client repo opens its DESIGN.md with a "creative north star" taken from the client's world: FBCM "the building, set on paper" (lancet-window and door photo frames, glyphs drawn from the sanctuary, the 1927 rendering), Stone Steps "objects on a ground" (tickets, punch card, record boards, hard offset shadow, mud, contours), Reid "the craft is the identity" (paint chips, fan deck, tape measure, sample tags). NCS has no DESIGN.md at all (Impeccable context reports `designPath: null`, "INCUMBENT_WORLD_UNDOCUMENTED"), and its system in `.claude/rules/styling.md` is tokens plus a list of effects (`.bg-aurora`, `.bg-mesh-soft`, `.bg-dotgrid`, `.band-themed`, `.shine`, spotlight cards, count-ups). Nothing in it comes from what Nathan does: a camera, a working session at a table, the one-page brief, the handoff. Evidence: `ncs/home__1440__light.png` (blue to white to blue, top to bottom), `ncs/home__1440__dark.png` (navy throughout), against `stonesteps/home__1440__light.png`, `reid/home__1440__light.png`, `A-shots/fbcm-new-home__1440__full.png`. Files: `src/styles/globals.css` (sections 1 and 6), `src/components/Hero.astro`.

2. **[P0] A photographer's site with no photographs where they matter.** `/photography` still ships zero photos and an "In progress" card under a navy gradient hero (`ncs/photography__1440__light.png`). `/services` illustrates Strategy and Photography with an empty dotted panel and a line icon (`ncs/services__1440__light.png`; `src/pages/services.astro` lines 86 to 89 and 291 to 320, `placeholderIcons`). The homepage hero is the WebGL colour field plus screenshots; the hero photo slot is still commented out (`Hero.astro` lines 58 to 59 and 96 to 98). All three client sites open on a real photograph of their real subject: a runner in Mt. Airy Forest, Staci at her work table, the FBCM sanctuary windows. NCS's only real photographs are on /about (`ncs/about__1440__light.png`), below the fold.

3. **[P1] The hero shows the work too small to read and, on phones, deliberately faded.** At 1440 the device scene renders client sites at roughly 40% scale, so their type is 6 to 8px and the screenshots read as texture (`ncs/hero-frames/home-hero__light__t3s.png`, `home-hero__dark__t8s.png`; in t8 half the browser frame is a flat navy slab mid-transition). On a phone the same scene becomes a backdrop at 0.5 opacity behind the headline, so the client's own text ("for the good of the world", button outlines) bleeds through behind the CTAs (`A-shots/ncs-live-home__390__fold.png`, `ncs/home__390__light.png`). The proof is literally faded out. Below the 624px hero the rest of the phone's first screen is blank white. Also: the scene currently leads with the Second Presbyterian site, which D found is not live. Files: `src/components/HeroShowcase.astro`, `Hero.astro` (`.hero-mobile-scene`).

4. **[P1] Four things move at once in the first viewport, and none of them is Nathan.** WebGL colour flow (cursor-reactive), two auto-scrolling screenshots, a new site sliding in every five seconds, and a scrolling client marquee, plus the headline entrance (in `hero-frames/home-hero__light__t0s.png` the headline is still mid-fade, grey and half-transparent). Compare FBCM: one cross-fading photo with a visible PAUSE control and a word rise on the headline (`A-shots/fbcm-new-home__1440__fold.png`); Reid: static; Stone Steps: one ticking race clock that carries real information (`stonesteps/home__1440__light.png`). NCS's motion is busy where theirs is restrained and meaningful. Files: `HeroCanvas.tsx`, `HeroShowcase.astro`, `ClientMarquee.astro`.

5. **[P1] Every section uses the same grammar, so the page has no rhythm.** Each homepage band is: Bebas h2 at 48px, a grey one-line subhead at about 42ch, left-aligned, then content, with the right half of the container empty (`ncs/home__1440__light.png`, crops of "What it costs" and "How we work"). Pricing is three equal columns, process is four equal columns with numbered circles on a rule, the CTA is a heading left and a button far right with 900px of nothing between. The client sites each break their own grid on purpose: Stone Steps' tickets in a frame, elevation chart, map, clipboard schedule, record boards; Reid's tape-measure process cards, the concept room with paint swatches, price tags; FBCM's arch-framed photos, hymn-board times, 1859 to 2026 timeline, glyph row. Files: `PricingTeaser.astro`, `ProcessBand.astro`, `SelectedWork.astro`.

6. **[P1] Light mode is one undifferentiated pale-blue field from the Process band to the bottom.** Process band, CTA and footer all use `.band-themed`, so in light mode roughly the last 1,700px of the homepage is the same blue-white wash with no seam (`ncs/home__1440__light.png`, bottom third). This is the June critique's "navy CtaBanner over navy footer" problem, solved for dark mode and recreated in light. The client sites change ground deliberately (Stone Steps paper, soft paper, bark; FBCM paper, brown, indigo, gold Give band; Reid linen, walnut, ink).

7. **[P1] The case studies are developer write-ups, not design showcases.** `ncs/work-second-presbyterian-chicago__1440__light.png`: the title is printed twice (h1 "Second Presbyterian Church of Chicago" then the same words again as the blue client line, because `[slug].astro` line 183 renders `client` under the title and they are identical here); metadata is set as mono uppercase "ROLE:", "BUILT WITH: ASTRO, SANITY, TAILWIND, CLOUDFLARE WORKERS", "TAGS:" plus pill badges (lines 173 to 252); the body is a narrow text column with mid-size screenshots and three small thumbnails; "What we built" ends in a nine-bullet feature list; "What changed" is a checkmark list with no numbers. There is a before/after slider, which is the best thing on the page. Nothing shows the client's people or place, no detail crop is large enough to see the craft (type, an arch frame, a ticket), and there is no single image that a church board member would remember. The client sites themselves are far more visual than the case studies that describe them.

8. **[P2] Developer-portfolio props aimed at the wrong buyer.** /about's terminal card (`$ whoami`, `$ npm run build`) and the Lighthouse score dial (`ncs/about__1440__light.png`, crop; `src/components/Terminal.astro`, `LighthouseScore.astro`) are the visual language of a front-end developer applying for a job. PRODUCT.md's buyer is a church board member or preschool director. None of the client sites use a terminal or score widget, even Stone Steps, which is the most technical of the three.

9. **[P2] Mono uppercase labels are still the section grammar on inner pages.** The June 21 audit asked to demote them to one earned use per page. They remain: case study (CHURCH · 3 MIN READ, OUTCOME, ROLE, BUILT WITH, TAGS, IN THIS CASE STUDY, A NOTE FROM THE DESIGNER), /about rail (ON SHORT, STORY, OUTSIDE, PRACTICE, NOW), /contact (ABOUT YOU, ABOUT THE PROJECT, ONE MORE THING), /work (PORTFOLIO, SECTOR), /services (WHAT'S INCLUDED). `font-mono` or `uppercase` appears 82 times across 27 source files. Reid's DESIGN.md bans tracked small-caps eyebrows outright; FBCM keeps them "rare: page openers and a few orienting labels".

10. **[P2] Unfinished surfaces in production.** /photography "In progress" card, /journal "The first entry is being drafted" card with a stray "NCS" heading (`ncs/journal__1440__light.png`), two placeholder panels on /services, and on /contact the "Where I am" map rendered as an empty grey box in the baseline capture (`ncs/contact__1440__light.png`; it is a keyless Google Maps iframe, `contact.astro` lines 481 to 493, so it may only have failed in the capture; not re-verified). Reid's PRODUCT.md names exactly this as an anti-reference: "an unfilled template section ... cost[s] trust faster than a plain photo does." Four "coming soon" surfaces on a one-person studio site read as a site that is not finished.

11. **[P2] /work is a uniform card grid after one featured card, then repeats itself.** One large Stone Steps card, then eight identical cards in three columns, then "Open any of them" listing the same nine sites again as a link table (`ncs/work__1440__light.png`). /services repeats the homepage's process band, pricing and Selected Work almost verbatim (`ncs/services__1440__light.png`), so a visitor who clicks Services from the homepage sees the homepage again.

12. **[P3] Footer watermark collides with the legal row.** The 22vw "NIXON CREATIVE STUDIO" watermark sits under and touches the copyright and Colophon / Privacy / Accessibility line on every page (`ncs/home__1440__light.png` crop, `ncs/photography__1440__light.png`, `ncs/journal__1440__light.png`). File: `src/components/Footer.astro`.

13. **[P3] The detector findings are minor.** Sub-agent B ran `impeccable detect --json` on `src/components`, `src/pages` and `src/layouts`: 4 findings. `side-tab` at `services.astro:563` (`.flow-card::after`) and `services.astro:732` (`.tier-card::after`), both 3px top accent bars, borderline (a top bar is not the side-stripe tell, but it is decoration on a card slab); `dark-glow` at `ComingSoon.astro:260` and `radial-halo` at `ComingSoon.astro:173`, real but on a page few visitors see. The detector found nothing on the homepage components. That is consistent with item 1: the site passes the mechanical anti-pattern checks and still reads as generic, because the problem is the absence of an idea, not the presence of banned patterns.

## 2. What the three client sites do that NCS does not

**One concept from the client's own world, applied everywhere.**

- FBCM: the church's lancet window and Adams Street door become photo frames (arch-masked photos with a gold mould line in "What to expect" and "The Church App"), four line glyphs drawn from the building mark the goals, the 1927 rendering inks into the history band (`A-shots/fbcm-new-home__1440__full.png`).
- Stone Steps: the race's objects are the components: a ticket per distance with a perforation and corner flash, a gun-time clock, a record board, a clipboard schedule, a LiDAR elevation profile, a course map you can hover to run (`stonesteps/home__1440__light.png`, `stonesteps/inner1-course__1440__light.png`).
- Reid: the tape measure is the process timeline, the concept room comes with live paint-chip swatches, every button is a notched sample tag with a punched hole (`reid/home__1440__light.png`, crops).
- NCS: browser chrome and a phone frame, which every web studio uses.

**The subject's real photograph leads.** Runner on the trail, Staci with a coffee and a fan deck, the sanctuary's stained glass. NCS leads with a colour field.

**A warm, owned ground and a palette with more than one hue.** Paper and bark with rust and forest (Stone Steps), paper and ink with indigo, gold, brown, taupe (FBCM), a seven-step bronze paint strip (Reid). NCS is one blue on white (light) or navy (dark); amber appears only on the dark hero accent.

**Two type voices.** Reid: light serif with true italic plus one hand-lettered word per page. FBCM: titling capitals over an italic serif lede. Stone Steps: solid display headings paired with outlined ghost section labels ("THE CLIMBING", "THE MAP"). NCS: Bebas caps over a neutral sans, with no italic or contrasting voice anywhere.

**Information as the graphic.** Stone Steps' elevation chart and records board, FBCM's service-time facts row on a hairline and its 1859 to 2026 timeline, Reid's prices on tags. NCS has strong raw material for this (prices, a four-step process with real durations, a one-page brief, Lighthouse numbers, the stack of real sites) but sets it all as plain columns.

**One art-directed theme.** FBCM retired dark mode on 2026-09-24 and Reid followed ("the FBCM precedent"). Both say a single theme let them art-direct. NCS maintains two first-class themes, which roughly doubles the work and is part of why both come out as a token swap of blue.

**Rules against the exact tells NCS still shows.** Reid's DESIGN.md: "No decorative numbering", "No tracked small-caps eyebrows". FBCM's: "One grammar per page", flat, no card shadows, motion only where a page opens. NCS keeps numbered process circles, mono eyebrows, card shadows and four simultaneous hero motions.

**Length and density earned by content.** Each client homepage is about 10,000px of distinct bands; NCS's is 5,362px of similar ones.

Caveat in NCS's favour: Reid's /portfolio is itself a "Case studies are on the way" holding page (`reid/inner1-portfolio__1440__light.png`), so case-study presentation is not something the client sites solve either. Their advantage is the homepage and art direction, not portfolio depth.

## 3. Keep (already good)

- **Leading /work and Selected Work with Stone Steps.** It is the strongest, most distinctive piece and the asymmetric one-large, two-medium layout on the homepage is right (`ncs/home__1440__light.png`).
- **Price transparency and the hairline tier layout** (not a card slab) on the homepage, with the Signature tier anchored. Reid proves that saying the price out loud is a design asset.
- **The process content itself** (one-hour meeting, written strategy doc, preview links, a month of follow-through). The content is specific; only its presentation is generic.
- **The /about story and its real photographs** (family, race day, wedding, portrait). This is the warmest material on the site and it is buried on page three.
- **The before/after slider on case studies** and the "A note from the designer" closer.
- **The engineering floor:** AA token split (`--link` vs `--accent`), reduced-motion handling everywhere, `.js`-gated reveals, server-rendered hero copy for LCP, three-state theme without flash. Keep this rigor whatever the new look is.
- **/contact's "What happens next" list and the plain form.** Calm and clear.
- **The footer "Currently: Booking projects" status.** Human and specific.

## 5. What a design must do to reach the client-site bar

1. **Pick one idea from Nathan's own working world and make it the system,** the way the building, the race and the work table work for the clients. Candidates the evidence supports: the photographer's contact sheet and darkroom marks (frames, grease-pencil crop marks, frame numbers as real facts, not decoration); the one-page brief as a physical document; the site plan or wireframe sheet. Write it down as a DESIGN.md with a north star, as every client repo has, before any component is drawn.
2. **Lead with a real photograph Nathan took,** of a client's place or people (or of Nathan at work), full-bleed or framed by the chosen idea. Ship /photography with real frames or remove it from the nav until it has them; replace the two service placeholder panels with photographs.
3. **Show the client work large enough to see the craft.** One site at a time, at a size where its type is legible, or tight crops of the details that make each site distinct (an FBCM arch frame, a Stone Steps ticket, a Reid paint chip). Never fade it behind text. Replace the Wix-era FBCM card with the new build once it launches.
4. **Give the type a second voice** (a serif or italic against the display face, or replace Bebas with a face that carries character on its own) and set the faces by role, as FBCM and Reid do.
5. **Turn the facts into the graphics.** The four-step process with real durations, the three prices, the one-page brief, real outcomes once D's proof gaps are closed. Stone Steps' elevation chart is the model: content that is also the picture.
6. **Vary the bands and the grounds.** Each fold one job and one distinct composition; change the ground deliberately between bands (a warm paper, a dark band, a photo band) instead of one blue wash.
7. **Spend motion once.** One opening move tied to the idea (for example a photo developing in, or a crop mark drawing), a pause control on anything that loops, nothing that moves while the visitor reads. Drop the WebGL flow and the auto-scrolling screenshots unless the new idea needs them.
8. **Make case studies visual:** one large hero image of the shipped site in context, three or four detail crops at legible size, the before/after slider kept, the client's place or people photographed, facts set as a short ledger instead of mono metadata, and no duplicated title.
9. **Remove every unfinished surface before launch** (journal card, photography card, placeholder panels, empty map box) and drop developer props (terminal, score dial) from /about in favour of Nathan's photographs and story.
10. **Consider a single art-directed theme,** following the FBCM and Reid decisions, so the effort goes into one excellent look rather than two blue variants. This is Nathan's call; the evidence from his own two sites favours it.
