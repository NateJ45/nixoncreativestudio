    Why one person

    Plenty of people will quote you less. Here is what the price actually buys, and what a cheaper one usually leaves out.

    • One person, no handoffs

      The person who plans your site designs it, builds it, and, when needed, shoots the photos. No agency markup, and nothing lost in translation between a strategist, a designer, and a developer who never quite talk to each other.

    • Built to be handed off

      Every site is set up so the next volunteer or staff member can keep it running, and you own it outright. A cheaper site that leaves you stranded the day the freelancer goes quiet costs far more to replace than it saved.

    • Accessible by default

      Every page is built to WCAG AA and checked until it scores 100 for accessibility in Lighthouse. Plenty of budget builds never get checked at all. For a church, school, or nonprofit it is the right thing to do, and it is included, not an upsell.

    • Cheap is expensive later

      A bargain site quietly runs into thousands a year once you bolt on the features it could not handle up front. I would rather build it once, on a stack that is nearly free to run and is yours to keep.

    I build with modern tools, including AI-assisted workflows, the same way I use frameworks and a camera. They help one person do the work of a team. The strategy, the design, and the accountability stay mine, start to finish.

    How it works

    1. Start with a conversation

      It starts with an hour together, in person or on video, talking about what you actually need. No pitch deck, no agency theater. Just a working session that ends with a one-page brief you sign off on.

    2. Then the strategy

      I write the audience map, the page architecture, and the conversion path. You read it and push back, and I revise. The strategy doc is the source of truth for every design choice that follows.

    3. Then I design and build

      Components, content, and pages built in the open. Preview links land in your inbox at every milestone so you can react early instead of seeing the whole thing for the first time at launch.

    4. Launch, and follow-through

      I launch the site with you, then stay close for the first month to fix anything that surfaces. After that you own the site outright, with documentation and a simple way to reach me when something comes up.

    Common questions

    What does it cost?

    Websites start at $4,000 for a focused Launch site. Most projects land between $7,000 and $11,000, and builds with real custom functionality start around $12,000. Every build is custom, comes with a content system you can manage yourself, and includes year one hosting; what moves the number is scope, functionality, photography, and content. The first conversation is free and ends with an honest ballpark before anyone is committed, and larger projects can be split into monthly payments so the budget is about cash flow, not one big wall.

    What if I have been quoted less somewhere else?

    You probably can find a cheaper quote, and for a very simple need that might be the right call. What a low quote usually leaves out is the part that matters later: accessibility, a content system your team can actually run, a plan for who maintains the site after launch, and someone who still answers the phone in a year. A site you have to rebuild in eighteen months is the expensive one. I would rather build it once.

    How long does a typical project take?

    Most websites land between six and ten weeks from kickoff to launch. Larger projects with custom photography or multiple content collections can run twelve to sixteen weeks. The brief at the end of strategy sets the real timeline.

    Do I need a care plan after launch?

    Year one hosting is included in every build. After that, every live site needs Hosting at the least, and I quote care with every build. Your site is yours outright, but hosting, security patches, domains, and certificates all keep moving, and a site nobody is watching tends to break quietly and at a bad moment. Hosting is $180 a year and covers hosting, uptime monitoring, daily backups, and renewals, with changes billed at $85 an hour. If you would rather not make edits yourself, Light Care ($49 a month), Care ($99), and Care+ ($149) add monthly edit time, software updates, and faster replies. Unused hours do not roll over, and you can change or cancel with 30 days notice.

    Can you work with my existing brand?

    Yes. If you already have a logo, palette, and type system, I use it. If you do not, brand work can be folded into the strategy phase or scoped separately.

    Where are you based, and do you work with clients outside Cincinnati?

    I'm based in Cincinnati, but web design, strategy, and brand work happen with clients anywhere, fully remote when that is easier. Photography is the one piece that needs me on site, so that stays within the greater Cincinnati region.
