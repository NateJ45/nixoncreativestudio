Church websites, starting with the first Sunday.

Every church site I build starts with the person who has never been inside: when the service is, how to get there, and what happens when they walk in. Then it has to work for the people who keep it running, so the staff can post the week’s news without waiting on a developer.

What a church site has to do

Make the first visit easy

A first-time visitor should know the time, the place and how the morning runs before they arrive. So the service times, the address and a plain what-to-expect page come first, ahead of everything the members already know.

Let the staff run it

I match the platform to whoever keeps the site up after launch: a custom build with an editing studio and a live preview for one church, Wix for another whose communications team wanted to own every page. Either way, the bulletin, the events and the news go up without me.

Cover the rest of the week

A church building is busy all week, and a church has a history worth keeping. On the sites below that meant one form for booking the building, a page about the building itself, and an old blog brought across with every post at its address.

Open to everyone

Every page I build is checked against WCAG AA, and that work is part of the price, not an upsell. For a church it is simply the right thing to do.
