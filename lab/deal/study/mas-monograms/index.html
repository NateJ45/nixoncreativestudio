The brief

A home embroidery studio moved off Squarespace onto a site Mary Ann owns, where a customer can type their initials, watch them stitch, and send that design into a quote.

MAS Monograms is a home-based custom embroidery studio run by Mary Ann Stone in St. Matthews, South Carolina. Every piece is made to order: the item, the font, the thread colors, the placement and the stitch count all change from one customer to the next, and each of those choices moves the price. The first site I built for her lived on Squarespace and worked around that with a quote-first flow: show the work, take a detailed request, then send a price.

She wanted to keep that flow, because it fits handmade work, and move everything else onto a site that was more her own: one she owns outright, one she can edit herself, and one that helps a customer picture a monogram before they ask for it.

  • Mary Ann owns her site now, built on Astro and served from Cloudflare.
  • A customer can design a monogram on the home page and send that exact design to Mary Ann as a quote request.
  • The home page, shop and gallery lead with real photographs of her finished work.
  • The quote form captures the item, placement, size, thread colors, font and reference photos up front, so requests arrive ready to price.
  • Every word and photo on the site lives in Sanity, so Mary Ann can change any of it herself.

Decisions

Keep the strategy, rebuild the foundation

The easy version
Stay on Squarespace and keep working inside its template.
What I did
Keep the quote-first flow, because for handmade embroidery it is still the honest way to set a price, and rebuild everything under it on Astro, Sanity and Cloudflare: static pages that belong to Mary Ann outright.

Let customers see it before they ask

The easy version
A row of category icons and a contact form.
What I did
A live Monogram Studio at the top of the home page. A customer types one to three initials, picks a style, a fabric and a thread, and watches the letters stitch themselves. When they like it, one button carries that design into the quote form.

The full build, with screenshots

Real work, grouped by item

The shop leads with photographs of Mary Ann's own finished pieces, one category per item, with starting prices, so a customer sees the stitching before they open the quote form.

A quote form built for custom work

Custom embroidery has no fixed cart price, so the site ends at a detailed request in four short parts: the order, the customer, optional photos and anything else. Mary Ann replies with a price within one business day, and nothing is stitched until it is approved.

A gallery that shows the range

Real finished pieces, from monograms to appliqué to pet portraits, filtered by item, theme, technique and recipient. Every piece has its own Request this link into the quote form.

The full build, including the pieces above:

  • Astro front end on Cloudflare, shipped as static pages Mary Ann owns
  • The Monogram Studio: five monogram styles, eight fabrics and 43 thread colors, drawn live as stitches, with a note that the lettering and colors are confirmed in a proof before anything is sewn
  • Shop organized into eight item categories, from tote bags and towels to baby gifts, each with real photography and starting prices, including a "Bring Your Own Item" path for a piece the customer already has
  • Style Gallery of real finished work with filters, each piece linked straight into the quote flow
  • Quote form covering the item, who supplies it, the monogram style, placement, size, thread colors, font, timeline and reference photos, checked by Cloudflare Turnstile, with every request backed up before it is emailed to Mary Ann
  • Font and Lettering Guide and a Thread Color Chart, so customers reach the form already fluent in the options
  • Pricing page with starting prices and add-ons, and a four-step How It Works: browse, request, quote, stitch
  • About page and a "Meet the maker" band telling Mary Ann's story: a home studio where every order comes straight to her
  • A new hoop-seal mark, a header that settles into a slim pill as you scroll, and a compact footer
  • Sanity Studio built into the site, with live preview, so every word and photo is Mary Ann's to change
  • Privacy, terms and accessibility pages

Behind the decisions

Everything a customer reads lives in Sanity, in a Studio built into the site at /studio. Mary Ann changes a photo, a price or a line of copy on the real page and publishes it herself, without a page builder and without waiting on me.
