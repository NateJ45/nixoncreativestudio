The brief

A site for a new Reformed lay formation school, laid out like a printed term catalog, that explains what the Academy is, who it is for and how to start.

The Presbyterian Academy is a Reformed lay formation school, made possible by the Presbytery of Cincinnati. Its courses are in person and in cohorts, for elders, teachers, small-group leaders and anyone who wants to study Reformed theology seriously without pursuing ordination.

A new school has a trust problem an established one does not. There is no alumni network to quote and no long history to point to. The site had to build credibility from what the Academy does have, its courses and the people teaching them, and it had to answer three questions in order: What is this? Is it for me? How do I start?

  • A new school has a front door that reads like its own term catalog: what the Academy is, every course and the people teaching it.
  • An interested visitor can size up the fit, get a suggested first course, then take a clear next step through a form, a free intro or a free class visit.
  • Every course has a cover and a page of its own, generated from the CMS, so a new course looks finished the day it is published.
  • The Academy team can update courses, faculty, terms and enrollment status from the Studio without involving a developer.
  • Rates and the scholarship note are published up front, with no "contact for pricing" hedging.

Decisions

Design it like the thing it is

The easy version
A generic school template with hero photography of empty lecture halls.
What I did
I designed the site as a printed term catalog, the Term Issue: a cover for the term, a contents page, course codes, contributors, letters and rates, set in Fraunces, Source Sans 3 and IBM Plex Mono on warm paper with a deep forest green. The school is text-forward, so the site is too.

The prices, in the open

The easy version
Bury the numbers and make people call.
What I did
Publish the rates and the scholarship note right in the issue. This audience is weighing a semester-long commitment, and they respect being given straight answers.

Help people pick a first course

The easy version
A list of courses and a contact form, and leave the choosing to the visitor.
What I did
A course finder styled as a reply card: three quick questions about what you do at church, what you want most and how much you can take on, and it suggests one place to start.

What I built

The full build, with screenshots

A catalog that answers 'is it for me?'

Each course shows its code, topic, teacher, term, sessions and enrollment status at a glance, with the question it asks and what week one covers, and the catalog filters by topic or teacher. A visitor can size up the fit without emailing to ask.

Three questions, one course

The finder asks three things and suggests a single course to begin with, with its teacher and rate, and a button to express interest in it.

The full build, including the pieces above:

  • Home page laid out as the term's issue: a cover with the term and its first class, a note before you browse, contents, the courses, contributors, letters, rates, a free trial evening, group enrollment and a look at the next issue
  • A slim bar across the top of every page with the current term's dates and an invitation to visit a class free
  • Course catalog with filters by topic and teacher, and a page per course with an at-a-glance table, the sessions, who it is for and a syllabus
  • Faculty directory with a profile for every teacher
  • Rates and need-based scholarship information, presented without "contact for pricing" hedging
  • A course finder that suggests one place to start
  • Request-information and express-interest forms that post to the site's own endpoint on Cloudflare, checked by Turnstile, with every submission stored before the Academy is emailed
  • A back-cover footer with the Academy's details and an A to Z index of the site
  • Light and dark themes
  • Full Sanity CMS for courses, faculty, terms, events and site settings

Behind the decisions

Every course gets a generated cover instead of stock photography: its code, its topic, its teacher and the one question the course is trying to answer. The covers are drawn from fields in the CMS, so a new course looks finished the moment it is published.

The CMS runs on Sanity, built into the site at /studio, so the Academy team can update courses, faculty, terms and enrollment status, including marking a course open or waitlisted, without involving a developer.
