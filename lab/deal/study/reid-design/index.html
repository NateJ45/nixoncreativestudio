The brief

A site for an Indianapolis-area interior designer that shows how she works, posts every price, and makes it simple to ask about a room.

Staci Perkins runs Reid Design out of Plainfield, Indiana, serving homeowners across the Greater Indianapolis suburbs with everything from a single consultation hour to full room design and styling.

The brief had two parts. First, show how Staci works and what it costs, so the people who reach out already know what to expect. Second, build something Staci can keep updated herself. Her ideal outcome was a site that does real qualification work before a lead ever calls her.

  • By the time someone books a consultation, they have seen how Staci works, what each service costs and what is in the plan, so the first call starts from shared understanding instead of square one.
  • The concept room shows how Staci thinks about a space, piece by piece, before anyone pays for a visit.
  • Every service price is on the site, so a visitor knows what working with Staci costs before they reach out.
  • Staci can change prices, pages and photos herself in Sanity, without touching a line of code.

Decisions

Answer the price question early

The easy version
Leave cost for the first call.
What I did
Post every price: the in-home consultation, E-Design, full room design, styling and sourcing, with design fees kept separate from what a client spends on furniture. Someone who has already seen the numbers arrives at the first call ready to talk about the room.

Show the thinking, not just the result

The easy version
A carousel of pretty rooms on the home page.
What I did
A concept room on the home page: one empty living room fills in as you scroll, and each piece is tied to a tag that says why it is there. It shows what Staci notices and how she plans, before anyone books.

The full build, including the pieces above:

  • Home page that leads with Staci, her Google rating and real reviews from Google and Facebook, then the four-step process with honest time estimates
  • The concept room, a scroll-built living room annotated with why each piece is there
  • Services page with every price posted, plus notes for builders and realtors and the service area
  • E-Design page for clients outside the drive radius: the same process, delivered online
  • Contact page with an optional room and style picker drawn as paint chips, and a link to book a free call first
  • A visual system taken from an interior designer's working day: paint chips, sample tags, fabric swatches and a tape measure
  • Full Sanity CMS for pages, services, photos and site-wide settings

Behind the decisions

Interior design clients almost always have two blockers early on: they do not know what things cost, and they do not know whether their taste matches what the designer actually does. I built around both. The prices are on the page, and the contact form asks which rooms and which paint-chip styles feel like home, from Transitional to Japandi, so Staci starts the first conversation already knowing the brief.

The site is built on Astro and Sanity, so Staci can change a price, a page or a photo herself, without touching a line of code.
