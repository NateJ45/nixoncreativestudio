A one-person studio for organizations that take their work seriously.

I'm Nathan Nixon. I run Nixon Creative Studio out of Cincinnati, Ohio, and I work with small businesses, nonprofits, churches and schools, in person around the region and anywhere for web work. I do the strategy, the design, the build and, when needed, the photography, so the person you meet on day one is the same person who hands you the finished site.

The longer story

I came to this work through a camera. For years I photographed portraits, families, weddings, the events people most want to remember, and the part I loved was helping someone show up at their best. When the shoots dried up in 2020, I started building websites for a few of those same clients, and it turned out to be the same job in a new medium: work out what someone is really trying to say, then make it clear. Web design is the studio's main work now, but the photographer's eye stayed, and I still shoot the photos when a project needs them.

Before any of that I studied mechanical engineering at the University of Edinburgh, back home in Scotland. The degree did not make me a designer, but it taught me to take a thing apart, understand why each piece is there, and put it back together so it holds. That is how I approach a website too: structure first, surface second, and built so the person who inherits it can actually keep it running.

I also do plenty of this work as a volunteer. I'm on the media team at Crestview Presbyterian Church, where I run the weekly livestream, plan and buy the equipment upgrades, and shoot the photo and video the church uses for its website and its socials.

I moved from Scotland to Cincinnati in 2017 to be with my wife, became a U.S. citizen in 2022, and these days the two of us are raising two kids here. When I'm away from a screen I'm usually out on a long run, chasing landscapes with a camera, deep in an ambitious Lego build with the kids, or a few episodes into a history drama.

What I do, and what I don't

I do

  • Plan, design and build your site myself, start to finish.
  • Shoot the photos when needed, around the greater Cincinnati region.
  • Use your existing brand, or fold brand work into the strategy.
  • Build every page to WCAG AA, because accessible is the default here.
  • Set it up so the next volunteer or staff member can keep it running.
  • Stay around after launch. Year one hosting is included, and I quote care with every build.

I don't

  • Pass you along. There's no strategist, designer and developer who never quite talk to each other.
  • Use a stock template. The design is built to your brand.
  • Lock you out of your own site. You own it outright.
  • Chase the lowest quote. For a very simple need, a cheaper one might be the right call.
  • Shoot photos farther afield. Web work happens anywhere, but photography needs me on site.

I build with modern tools, including AI-assisted workflows, the same way I use frameworks and a camera. The strategy, the design and the accountability stay mine, start to finish. What each project costs is on the prices page.
