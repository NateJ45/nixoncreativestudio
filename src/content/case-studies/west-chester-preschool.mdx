<Chapter title="The brief">

<Lede study={props.study} />

West Chester Preschool is a parent co-op preschool in West Chester, Ohio, founded in 1969. Families help in the classroom alongside two teachers, and the families also run the school, including everything the public sees. Volunteers change every year or two, so the public side of the school tends to get whatever time the current parents have left over.

When I took on publicity, the school was still called West Chester Cooperative Nursery School, its Instagram had about six posts spread over three years, there was nowhere to buy a school shirt, and the website had grown to 15 pages that were hard to edit. Parents choosing a preschool look at all of it, so I worked on all of it.

<Chapter title="Decisions">

<Decision
  title="A name parents would search for"
  easy="Keep the long legal name and add a tagline."
  did="Lead with West Chester Preschool, the words a parent actually types into a search box, and keep co-op in the logo so the thing that makes the school different is still front and centre."
/>

<Decision
  title="A store that runs itself"
  easy="Order a box of shirts once a year and sell them at pick-up."
  did="A print-on-demand store on Fourthwall at store.westchesterpreschool.org. Families order any size or color any time of year, every product is made when it sells, and nobody has to store, count or hand out stock."
/>

<Decision
  title="Social built from what happens in class"
  easy="Post a flyer whenever there is a fundraiser."
  did="Film and photograph real class days and events, then cut them into monthly recap reels and named series. Every piece carries the same fonts, colors and end card, and every one is checked so no child without a photo release appears."
/>

<Decision
  title="A website volunteers can keep"
  easy="Rebuild it on my own stack, the way I build client sites."
  did="Stay on Squarespace, which the school already uses, and rebuild every page from native blocks so text, photos and buttons all edit in the normal page editor. The finish is custom CSS where every rule follows a setting volunteers already use, like a section's color or whether a list shows cards, so a parent restyles a section by changing a setting, never by touching code. A short script adds behavior only, and the pages still work if it ever fails to load."
/>

<Decision
  title="Accessible on Squarespace's terms"
  easy="Write off the scan failures that come from Squarespace's own header and footer as platform problems."
  did="Fix them anyway. The two menus and the social links now have names a screen reader can tell apart, the background video has a pause button, and every animation switches off for anyone whose device asks for less motion. The list of accepted exceptions is empty."
/>

<Chapter title="Before and after">

<BeforeAfter

The old drawing has real charm, two children reading back to back, but a logo has to work on a phone screen, a shirt and a flyer taped to a door. This one struggled at every size the school actually uses:

- **It disappears when it gets small.** Thin hand-drawn lines and a ring of tiny lettering turn to grey fuzz as a social profile picture, a browser tab icon or a pocket print on a tee. The new mark is thick shapes and heavy letters that still read at the size of a thumbnail.
- **The name is the hardest part to read.** The school's name runs around a curve in small type, and the biggest letters on the logo are WCCNS, initials no parent searches for. The new logo puts West Chester Preschool front and center, in the words a family types into Google.
- **It has no color.** Black line art gave the school nothing to build on. The navy, orange and sky blue of the new logo became the colors of the website, the store, the social feed and every flyer.
- **It is hard to print and stitch.** Fine detail fills in on screen printing and can't be embroidered at a small size. The new mark has a few solid shapes, so it prints cleanly on all 49 store products and comes in navy and white versions for light and dark backgrounds.
- **It looks backward.** The illustration style and the old name read as a different era. A sun rising over clouds is warm and simple, and it says "a good start" without needing a caption. "Co-op" stays in the logo, because the parent co-op is what makes the school different.

Everything I made for the school:

- **Name and logo:** the move from West Chester Cooperative Nursery School to West Chester Preschool, and a reworked logo with white and navy versions for light and dark backgrounds
- **Online store:** 49 products across youth, women’s, men’s and accessories, each with the logo set up for its own colors and print area, on the school’s own store address
- **Social media:** monthly recap reels, event reels and fundraiser posts, built on a shared template so every piece looks like it came from the same school
- **Photography and video:** hundreds of photos and clips from class days, events and fundraisers, edited and handed over for the school to use in its advertising
- **Fundraising site:** a [flyer maker](https://wcpfundraising.pages.dev/popcorn/) for the popcorn sale, the flower sale and Dine to Donate at City BBQ, where each family makes its own print, feed or story flyer with a QR code that opens its own shop
- **Ads and inquiries:** Meta ad campaigns through enrollment season, Google Analytics and Search Console on the site, the Google, Apple Maps and Yelp listings brought up to date, and every inquiry form sent straight to the board's task board with an automatic reply to the family
- **Website:** cut from 15 pages to 7, with the class facts, the state license number and inspection link, a longer FAQ, search titles and descriptions on every page, and 10 old addresses redirected to the page that now answers them
- **Accessibility:** every text element and button, hover states included, meets its WCAG contrast level; the brand orange comes in two shades, a deeper one that carries white text and a bright one kept for decoration; no page scrolls sideways on a phone as narrow as 320 pixels; Lighthouse scores 100 for accessibility on every page
- **Search:** Lighthouse scores 100 for SEO, and the school's details (address, phone, founding year and Ohio license number) are marked up as Preschool and ChildCare data that passes the Schema.org validator with no errors or warnings

### Behind the decisions

The constraint that shaped everything was the next volunteer. Every choice had to pass one question: could a parent with no design background keep this going next year? That is why the store makes its own stock, the social pieces come from a template, and the website is all native blocks. The work I do for paying clients is the same, I just usually do it one piece at a time.

Speed is the one place the site is not where I would want it. Squarespace loads a lot of its own script, and the homepage leads with a video, so its Lighthouse speed score on a phone sits in the 40s, about where it was before I started. That is the trade for a site any parent can edit.
