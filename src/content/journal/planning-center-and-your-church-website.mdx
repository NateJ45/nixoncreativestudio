This is for the church administrator who runs Planning Center and has been asked to "put it on the website". I build church sites on WordPress and on a custom stack (Astro with Sanity), so I'll say where each fits. Everything below about Planning Center comes from its own pricing pages and help center, read in November 2026, and I link each one.

If you only want the giving button working, skip to the section on putting the giving form on your site. That one job covers most churches.

## What is Church Center?

Church Center is the free app and website your members already use to reach Planning Center. It is where people give, sign up for events, find a group, check the directory and pre-check children in.

Every church on Planning Center gets a Church Center address that ends in `.churchcenter.com`. You can change the first part to your church's name, but Planning Center's help center says the `.churchcenter` part [is not customizable](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139216-should-you-use-church-center-as-your-church-s-website-.html). That one fact is why most churches still keep their own website and point to Church Center from it.

Planning Center describes Church Center as able to work alongside your own site, handling the event lists, giving and directory while your website does the rest. It also says Church Center can serve as a simple website on its own, if you don't need your own domain or much design.

The directory and event request forms are link only too, which is fine, since both are for members who are already signed in.

## What does each one cost?

Each product has a free tier and then climbs in steps, priced by how much your church uses it.

| Product       | Priced by                        | Steps after free                |
| ------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |

| Registrations | Attendees at your largest signup | $15 (20) up to $239 (5,000)     |
| Groups        | People in active groups          | $15 (75) up to $239 (unlimited) |
| Calendar      | Rooms you manage                 | $15 (5 rooms) up to $239        |
| Publishing    | Features                         | Plus $15, Premium $32           |
| People        | Not priced                       | Free, unlimited                 |

Giving and paid Registrations also carry processing fees. In the U.S., Planning Center lists 2.15% plus 30 cents for a card, 30 cents for a bank (ACH) transfer, and an extra 1.5% for international cards. On a $100 card gift, that is $2.45.

Planning Center says there are no contracts or setup fees, the first charge comes after 30 days, and going over your plan once does not cut you off. If it happens more than once, your administrator gets an email suggesting a bigger plan.

## What are the two ways to put Planning Center on your site?

A plain link, or a popup that opens Church Center over your page. Planning Center calls the popup a modal.

**A link** is the simplest. It takes people from your site to Church Center, and you choose how the button looks. Giving links can do more than you'd think: Planning Center documents adding a specific fund, a suggested amount (`?default_amount=100`) or a recurring gift to the [donation link](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/144372-link-to-your-donation-form-on-your-website.html). A "Give to the building fund" button can open the form with that fund already picked.

**The popup** keeps people on your page. It needs two things: a script tag (`https://js.churchcenter.com/modal/v1`) in your site's header, and a link with `?open-in-church-center-modal=true` on the end. The same method works for [People forms](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139195-integrate-a-form-onto-your-website.html).

Planning Center lists the catches itself:

- Your site has to use https, or the form opens in a new tab instead.
- On phones the form always opens in a new browser window, because phones don't support the popup.
- The browser's back and forward buttons may not behave inside it.
- Apple Pay works in the popup only if your site matches the website listed on your Organization page in Planning Center's account settings.

That phone point matters more than it looks. Plenty of visitors come on phones, and for them the popup and the link end up doing the same thing.

## How do you embed the calendar?

From the Events page in Calendar: Actions, then Share events, then Embed. Planning Center offers the [calendar embed](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/137808-embed-church-center-calendar.html) as a script, a URL or an iframe.

You can choose a month view, a list or a gallery of upcoming events with pictures, and filter by campus or by Church Center category. Two things trip people up. Only events published to Church Center appear, so an event that is missing on the website is usually missing in Church Center first. And the month view needs a space wider than 720 pixels, so phones always get the list.

## What belongs on the website, and what belongs in Church Center?

The website is for people who don't know you yet. Church Center is for people who already belong.

| Keep on your website                          | Keep in Church Center               |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Service times, address, parking               | The giving form and giving history  |
| What a first visit is like                    | Event signups and payments          |
| Beliefs, staff, ministries for kids and youth | The group finder and group chat     |
| A short list of what's coming up              | The full calendar and room bookings |
| Recent sermons, in your own design            | The directory and profiles          |
| A plain giving page explaining why and how    | Check-in and volunteer schedules    |

<Aside>

Sources: my own split, based on Planning Center's [Should you use Church Center as your church's website?](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139216-should-you-use-church-center-as-your-church-s-website-.html) and its [Church Center page](https://www.planningcenter.com/church-center), read November 2026.

</Aside>

The rule I follow: write about a thing on the website, and do the thing in Church Center. A page about your youth group, with photos and a leader's name, sells it. The signup button then sends people to Registrations, where the form, the payment and the roster already live. Don't copy Church Center content onto the website by hand, or you'll be updating every event in two places.

One trap is event links. Planning Center notes that if an event or form is duplicated, its address changes. If you copy last year's Vacation Bible School signup to make this year's, update the website button too.

## How does it work on Squarespace?

You can link anything, and the popup and calendar need code.

Squarespace says [code injection](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815908-Using-code-injection) is available on its Core, Plus and Advanced plans and some older plans. That is where the popup's header script goes. Check your plan before you promise anyone a giving popup. Buttons that link to Church Center work on any plan, and since phones get a new window anyway, a well-placed "Give" button is often all you need.

## How does it work on WordPress?

The links and embeds go into a Custom HTML block, and the header script goes in through your theme or a small header-code plugin.

There is one setting worth knowing. WordPress's own help page on the [Custom HTML block](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/custom-html/) says that users without the `unfiltered_html` capability, a Contributor for example, have script and iframe tags stripped when they save. So if a volunteer pastes the calendar embed and it disappears, that's why. Have an administrator add the embed once, on a page volunteers don't need to edit.

## How does it work on a custom build?

The same links and embeds, set up once and styled to match the rest of the site.

On a custom build I put the popup script on every page, so any "Give" or "Sign up" button can open Church Center without anyone touching code. The editor can then have a plain field for a Church Center link on an event or ministry page, which is much harder to break than a pasted embed.

A custom site can go further with Planning Center's API. Its help center says [the API](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/144877-the-planning-center-api.html) covers Calendar, Check-Ins, Giving, Groups, People and Services, so a site could show upcoming events in its own design instead of in the embedded calendar. Registrations and Publishing are not on that list, so signups and sermons from Publishing stay as links. The API uses access tokens tied to your church's account, which have to stay on the server and never sit in a web page.

Embeds and links fit any of my tiers, starting with Launch at $3,500, then $200 a year for hosting from year two. A live connection to Planning Center's data is the kind of work in Flagship, which I quote from $12,500 by scope.

## What I'd do

For most churches: a clear "Give" button that opens the giving form, the calendar embedded on an events page, and a link for each group, signup and form. That costs nothing beyond the Planning Center plans you already pay for.

I'd spend the effort on the website side instead. Write the first-visit page, the kids' page and a giving page that explains how gifts are used, then link from each to the right place in Church Center.

## Do I need Publishing Plus for my website?

No. Publishing changes Church Center's own pages, not your website. The free Essential tier is enough if your website carries the content.

## Can I embed group signups or event registrations?

Not as a popup. Planning Center lists Groups and Registrations signups as link only, so a button that opens them in Church Center is the way.

## Can Church Center use my church's domain?

No. Church Center addresses always end in `.churchcenter.com`. You can change the part before it to your church's name.
