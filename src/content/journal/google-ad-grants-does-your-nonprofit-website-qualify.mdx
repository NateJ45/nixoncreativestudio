This is for small nonprofits, ministries and charities in the United States who have heard about "free Google ads" and want to know whether their website will get them turned down. It covers the website side in detail and the account rules briefly. It does not cover how to write good ads or pick keywords. If your organization is a school, a hospital or a government body, read the eligibility section first, because you may not qualify at all.

Everything here comes from Google's own help pages, which I read in November 2026. I link each one. Google updates these pages, so check them again before you apply.

## What is Google Ad Grants?

Free search ads for qualifying nonprofits, up to $10,000 a month.

The [Google Ad Grants page](https://www.google.com/grants/) says each qualifying nonprofit has access to up to $10,000 a month in search ads shown on Google.com. Google's [budgets and bidding page](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en) says the monthly limit works out to a daily budget of $329, and is careful to call it a limit, not a promise that you will spend it. In practice how much you spend depends on how many people search for what you offer and how good your ads are.

## Who is eligible?

In the U.S., a registered 501(c)(3) that passes Google's validation, and is not a school, hospital or government body.

Google's [U.S. eligibility guidelines](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en) say you must be registered with the IRS as exempt under 501(c)(3), or be part of a group exemption with proven affiliation to a central 501(c)(3). You must also be verified by Google's validation partner, Goodstack, and agree to Google for Nonprofits' additional terms.

The same page lists who is not eligible: government entities, hospitals and health care organizations, and schools, academic institutions and universities. The charitable arms or foundations of hospitals, and the philanthropic arms of educational organizations, are eligible. Fiscally sponsored groups without their own 501(c)(3) status are not.

### Can churches apply?

Churches are not on Google's list of ineligible organization types, and Google's [account management policy](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827) even uses houses of worship as an example of an organization that should target its ads locally. The catch is tax status. Under [IRS rules](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizations-not-required-to-file-form-1023), many churches are tax exempt without ever applying for a 501(c)(3) determination letter, and Google's U.S. page asks for IRS registration. If your church has never applied for that letter, check whether Goodstack can verify you without one before you count on the grant. I could not find an official Google page that sets out how ads with religious content are treated under Ad Grants, so check the current Google Ads policies before you plan your campaigns.

### What about church-run preschools and schools?

Schools and academic institutions are on the ineligible list. A separate foundation or philanthropic arm may qualify. If your preschool is a ministry of the church rather than its own organization, ask Google before you apply; I have not seen an official answer that covers that case.

## What does Google require of the website?

An owned domain, HTTPS, real content, a clear mission, working navigation, fast and mobile-friendly pages, and limited commercial activity.

This is where most applications are won or lost, and it is the part I can actually help with. Google's [website policy](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1657899?hl=en) lists what it checks and the common reasons it turns sites down.

| Requirement         | What Google asks for                                                               | Quick check                                                |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owned domain        | You own the domain the ads point to; no redirect to a third-party platform         | Your own address, not a social page or donation portal     |
| HTTPS               | Whole site on HTTPS, valid certificate, no mixed content, HTTP redirects           | Padlock on every page, including old ones                  |
| Substantial content | Original content about your mission; no thin, copied or "under construction" pages | Home, About, Mission, Programs, Contact all have real text |
| Clear mission       | Mission stated prominently, programs and who you serve described                   | Address and EIN or annual report visible                   |
| Navigation          | Clear menu, no broken links or empty pages                                         | Donate and contact buttons both work                       |
| Speed and mobile    | Fast on all devices, responsive design                                             | Run PageSpeed Insights on Mobile                           |
| Commercial activity | Mission first; no AdSense or affiliate links; secure donation page                 | Any shop explains how sales fund the work                  |

Sources: [Google for Nonprofits website policy](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1657899?hl=en), [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).

### The common reasons sites are rejected

Google's website policy lists seven: insufficient unique content, an unclear mission, poor navigation or broken links, slow load speed, no HTTPS, disallowed commercial activity, and a domain redirect. Almost every one of those is a website problem, not an ads problem.

The two that catch small organizations most often, in my reading of the policy, are the redirect and the thin content. If your web address forwards to a page on a donation platform or a social network, Google wants the domain to host your own site instead. And a site that is mostly a few sentences, a PDF newsletter and a "Donate" button does not count as substantial content. The policy also warns against relying heavily on PDFs, so put your programs and annual report on real pages.

### Speed

The policy points you to the Mobile score in [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). It does not name a pass mark, so I would not trust anyone who quotes one as an official rule. Aim to fix whatever the report flags as slowest, usually oversized photos and too many plugins or scripts.

## What are the rules once you are approved?

Keep a 5% click-through rate each month, track at least one real conversion, and answer Google's survey.

The [account management policy](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827) says accounts that are not using only Smart campaigns must keep a 5% click-through rate each month, measured across the whole account. Miss it two months in a row and the account is temporarily deactivated until you fix it and ask to be reinstated. The policy also asks you to target ads only where your services are useful, include at least two sitelinks, set up at least one meaningful conversion (a donation, a sign-up, a volunteer form) and record at least one conversion a month, use one of the allowed Smart Bidding strategies, and respond to the program survey Google sends.

| Account rule         | What it means for the website                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 5% CTR each month    | Pages must match what the ad promises            |
| At least 2 sitelinks | You need several solid pages to link to          |
| 1 conversion a month | A working form or donation page Google can track |
| Local targeting      | Your location and service area must be clear     |

Source: [Ad Grants account management policy](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827).

## What I'd do

I would fix the website first and apply second. Work through the website policy table with your own site open on your phone. If you are on your own domain, fully on HTTPS, with real pages for your mission, programs and contact details, you are in good shape. If your domain redirects somewhere else, or the site is a single page and a PDF, start there.

## Does Google pay me $10,000 in cash?

No. It is ad credit for search ads shown on Google, up to $10,000 a month, not money paid to your organization.

## Can I use a donation platform as my website?

Not as the place your ads point to. The website policy says your nonprofit must own the domain your ads point to, and that the domain cannot redirect to a third-party platform or donation portal. A donate button that leads to a secure donation page is fine.

## Do I need a new website to qualify?

Not always. Many existing sites only need HTTPS fixed, a redirect removed, broken links cleaned up and a few thin pages filled out. A full rebuild makes sense when the site is slow, hard to update, or cannot be fixed without one.
