This is for the executive director, development lead or board member who has been asked what a new website would cost, and whether a funder might pick up the bill. I've put the public prices side by side, added up five years of each, included the card fees people forget, and looked at which kinds of grants actually cover a website.

If your nonprofit already has a site that loads on a phone, says plainly what you do, and takes a gift without fuss, you may not need a new one. Fixing the content and the donate page is often the cheaper move.

## What are the ways to get a nonprofit website?

You build it yourself, you rent a fundraising platform and pair it with a simple site, or you pay someone to build one for you.

A **DIY builder** (Squarespace, Wix, WordPress.com) gives you templates and a monthly bill. You do the design, the writing and the upkeep. Two of the three offer something off for nonprofits.

A **fundraising platform** (Givebutter, Bloomerang, DonorPerfect) is mostly a donor database with donation forms and campaign pages. None of the three I looked at sells itself as a full website builder, so most organizations use one alongside a site, not instead of it.

A **custom build** means a designer or developer makes the site around your organization, and then you pay for hosting and, if you want it, ongoing help.

## Do DIY builders give nonprofits a discount?

Wix does, Squarespace gives a small one, and I couldn't find one for WordPress.com.

**Wix** has the biggest offer. Its [help page on the nonprofit discount](https://support.wix.com/en/article/premium-plan-discount-for-charities-or-non-profit-organizations) says TechSoup members get 70% off a two-year Premium plan for one site, plus a free one-year domain voucher. You request the coupon through [TechSoup](https://www.techsoup.org/products/wix-premium-core-plan-g-51063-), which lists it for 501(c)(3) organizations and public libraries. Read the small print: after two years the plan renews at the standard rate unless you cancel or apply again, and you can request one code every two years.

**Squarespace** has no nonprofit plan. Its own [guide to building a nonprofit site](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015429248-Building-a-nonprofit-site) says the code NONPROFIT takes 10% off your first payment. Its [donation blocks page](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206543967-Donation-blocks) is clear that transaction fees aren't discounted for nonprofits.

**WordPress.com** lists no nonprofit price that I could find. Its [pricing page](https://wordpress.com/pricing/) puts the Business plan at $25 a month billed yearly.

At full price, as of November 2026, the [Squarespace pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing) lists Core at $29 a month billed yearly, and the [Wix plans page](https://www.wix.com/plans) shows Core at $29.77 a month on a yearly plan.

To make that concrete: at 2.9% plus 30¢, a $100 gift costs $3.20 to process. If you raise $50,000 a year online in $100 gifts, that is $1,600 a year in fees, which is more than most DIY plans cost. Applying for Stripe's or PayPal's nonprofit rate, or asking donors to cover the fee, is worth the half hour it takes.

## Can a grant pay for a website?

Sometimes. The money usually comes from capacity-building grants or general operating support, rarely from a program grant.

**Capacity-building grants** pay to strengthen how an organization runs: systems, staff skills, technology. Some funders name websites outright. The [Sisters of Charity Foundation of Canton](https://www.scfcanton.org/wba/news/sisters-of-charity-foundation-seeking-applicants-for-capacity-building-grants/), for nonprofits in five Ohio counties, offered up to $15,000 per proposal in its 2023 capacity-building call, and gave website upgrades and web development as examples of the projects it funds. Candid's [piece on technology funding](https://candid.org/blogs/how-to-get-technology-funding-nonprofit-tech-grants/) names the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation's capacity-building grants, which include technology.

**General operating support** is unrestricted money, so you can spend it on a website if your board agrees. It is the easiest grant to use this way and the hardest to win.

**Federal awards** treat a website as an overhead cost in most cases. Since the [2024 revision of the federal grant rules](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/22/2024-07496/guidance-for-federal-financial-assistance), an organization without a negotiated indirect rate can charge a de minimis rate of up to 15% of modified total direct costs, up from 10%, and that overhead can help cover shared costs like a website.

**Google for Nonprofits** is not a cash grant. The [Google for Nonprofits FAQ](https://www.google.com/nonprofits/about/faq/) lists no-cost Workspace and other tools, and [Google Ad Grants](https://www.google.com/grants/) gives up to $10,000 a month in search ads. It won't pay for the site, but it does expect a good one: I wrote up the rules in [Google Ad Grants: does your nonprofit website qualify?](/journal/google-ad-grants-does-your-nonprofit-website-qualify/).

Funding for technology is not getting easier. The same Candid article reports that the share of grantmakers providing tech tools fell from 23% in 2022 to 20% in 2024.

## What do funders want to see?

A link to your mission, a clear need, a realistic budget and a way to measure the result.

Candid's article, written by the head of the Technology Association of Grantmakers, gives three steps: say what the investment does for your mission, show the need with real examples of what isn't working, and fit the ask to each funder's priorities. TechSoup's [advice on what funders look for](https://blog.techsoup.org/en-us/posts/what-do-funders-look-for-in-a-grant-proposal) adds a work plan and budget that line up and can deliver within the grant period, often one year.

For a website, that might mean:

- **The need**: volunteers can't sign up on a phone, or the program pages are three years out of date.
- **The outcome**: more online gifts, more sign-ups, fewer calls asking for basic information.
- **The measure**: numbers you can report a year later, like form completions or online gift totals.
- **The plan after the grant**: who edits the site, and how you will pay for hosting in year two.

That last one matters. A funder paying for a build will want to know the site won't go stale when the grant ends. With my prices, that answer is $200 a year for Hosting from year two.

## Can a grant pay for hosting and care too?

Sometimes, for the grant period. Ongoing costs usually come back to your operating budget, so plan for them.

## Do these prices include a domain name?

Not in my five-year table. Wix's discount includes a one-year domain voucher. On my Hosting plan, I handle the renewal for you.

## Is a fundraising platform enough on its own?

For taking gifts, it can be. For explaining your programs and passing Google's Ad Grants website checks, you still want a site on your own domain.
